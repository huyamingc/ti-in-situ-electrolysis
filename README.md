# Bimetallic Complementary Reduction Titanium Production Process — Computational Study

This repository contains the manuscript and all Python scripts for the paper

**"Electrochemical Design Basis for Titanium Production by Mg–Ca Co-Deposition in a Quaternary Chloride Melt"**

prepared for submission to the *Journal of The Electrochemical Society (JES)*, Electrochemical Engineering area. This repository now holds the **post-review revision** (54 pages): the mechanism has been rebuilt around the Ca + MgO -> CaO + Mg reaction with Case I / Case II carried through, engineering content (flowsheet, settled-bed reactor, semi-continuous operation, electrostatic separation) has been added, the uncertainty analysis has been expanded to eleven parameters, and results are reported at both 600 and 620 C. See `review_workspace/response_and_resubmission_v1.md` for the point-by-point response. The manuscript uses the standard `article` class with double spacing and embedded `thebibliography` (ECS reference format), and compiles with `pdflatex` alone.

## Key Results (600 °C design-basis temperature)

- Reduction thermodynamics: ΔG° = −231.0 to −307.5 kJ/mol for the three pathways (Mg, Ca, and combined Mg + Ca)
- Deoxidation limit: Mg → 3908 ppm O, Ca → 20.2 ppm O (193× difference); equimolar Mg–Ca alloy → ≈77 ppm O (design-relevant value)
- Ca²⁺/Na⁺ co-deposition window: +120.2 mV (ideal), 74.9–147.1 mV under activity-coefficient sensitivity
- Cathode mass transport: j_lim,Mg = 0.56 A/cm², co-deposition regime 0.56–1.31 A/cm², equimolar-alloy design point j ≈ 1.1 A/cm², ohmic budget L ≲ 1.1 cm
- Mg–Ca liquid alloy: ΔG_mix = −9.69 kJ/mol (equimolar)
- Shrinking-core kinetics: 100 μm particles reduced in 6.3 min (D = 2.0×10⁻⁹ m²/s)
- **Ca reduces MgO**: ΔG°(Ca + MgO → CaO + Mg) = −38.2 kJ/mol at 600 °C, K = 194; consequently Ca is the
  thermodynamic deoxidation driver and Mg's role is to keep the reductant *liquid* at 600 °C (both pure metals are
  solid there; the Mg–Ca eutectic is ≈517 °C)
- **New design criteria**: the Ca-vs-MgO crossover is identically the Mg-only deoxidation limit (3,910 ppm), and
  continuing below it requires a_MgO < 5 × 10⁻³
- Net energy consumption: 12,552 kWh/ton-Ti (Case II: MgO + CaO regenerated) or 12,933 kWh/ton-Ti (Case I: CaO the
  sole regenerated species, +3%); conditional on η_I > 0.70
- Monte Carlo, eleven parameters (N = 10⁵, seed = 42): median 13,141 kWh/ton-Ti on the 1:1 basis and 13,536 on the CaO-only basis, with P(W_net < 15,000) = 82.1% and 76.6% respectively; with the
  η–j correlation included, median 13,981 and P = 65.9%; joint feasibility at 620 °C = 95.8%
- SCM time, eleven-parameter median 13.6 min (the nominal 6.3 min corresponds to c_red = 3 mol%)
- Required interelectrode gap for the equimolar deposit: median 0.84 cm, P95 1.88 cm
- 620 °C (pilot floor): window 119.6 mV (from 120.2 mV), energy change < 0.3%
- Cell architecture: Ti (4.5 g/cm³) settles through the melt (1.8 g/cm³) at ≈5 mm/s, and the
  Mg–Ca alloy (≈1.6 g/cm³) is lighter than the melt, so the product cannot be held in
  suspension; the design uses a settled TiO₂ bed on a sloped floor as the cathode, with the
  reductant electrodeposited within the bed and retained by capillarity, a shroud-isolated anode
  and semi-continuous gravity drainage
- Separation: the product inherits the precursor particle size, so screening cannot separate Ti
  from unreacted TiO₂; dry electrostatic separation (conductor vs insulator) is the primary step,
  with the oxide fraction returned to the cell

## Limitations

The following items cannot be resolved by computation and require specialized software or experimental data:

1. CALPHAD database validation (liquidus + exact activity coefficients)
2. Electrode kinetic parameters j₀ / α (requires cyclic voltammetry)
3. Pilot-scale experimental validation (current efficiency, product purity, anode lifetime)
4. Impurity separation energy (requires experimental impurity phase distribution)
5. Current-efficiency verification (requires pilot electrolysis cell measurement)

The current-efficiency assumption (η_I = 0.75–0.80) is an unvalidated hypothesis anchored to the Hall–Héroult,
Downs, and industrial MgCl₂-electrolysis analogies, and is the dominant uncertainty in the energy estimate
(Monte Carlo Spearman ρ = −0.85). Adding the η–j correlation raises the median net consumption to 13,981 kWh/ton-Ti
and lowers P(W_net < 15,000) to 65.9%.

The two quantities that cannot be computed — the 600 °C CaO solubility and the interfacial kinetics of
Ca + MgO → CaO + Mg — are identified as the pivotal measurements, and go/no-go criteria are stated so that the
design basis can be falsified by a small number of experiments.
