This work establishes an electrochemical design basis for titanium production by in situ co-deposition of Mg and Ca, cyclic complementary reductants of TiO2, in a quaternary chloride melt (MgCl2-CaCl2-NaCl-KCl). Thermodynamic calculations at 600 °C give ΔG° = −231.0 to −307.5 kJ/mol for the reduction pathways, with equilibrium deoxidation limits of 3908 ppm O (Mg), 20.2 ppm (Ca), and ≈77 ppm (equimolar alloy). The Ca2+/Na+ co-deposition window is 120.2 mV (ideal) and remains positive (74.9–147.1 mV) for γ_CaCl2 = 0.3–1.0. Mass-transport analysis yields a Mg limiting current density of 0.56 A/cm²; co-deposition spans 0.56–1.31 A/cm², the equimolar deposit requires j ≈ 1.1 A/cm², and the 0.9 V ohmic budget constrains the gap to ≤1.1 cm. The shrinking-core model predicts complete reduction of 100 μm particles in 6.3 min. At the nominal design point (η_I = 0.75, V_cell = 4.1 V), the net equivalent energy consumption is 12,552 kWh/ton-Ti, conditional on achieving η_I > 0.70. Monte Carlo analysis (η_I ~ Tri(0.50, 0.75, 0.88)) gives a median of 13,141 kWh/ton-Ti and P(W < 15,000) = 82.1%. All results are theoretical; viability hinges on MgO/CaO solubility ≥0.5 wt% at 600 °C, melt oxoacidity favoring O2 over Cl2 evolution, and a durable oxygen-evolving inert anode.
